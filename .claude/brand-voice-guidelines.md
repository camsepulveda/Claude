## Executive Summary

TCT Oncology is a bone marrow transplant and cellular therapy program based at Hospital Auxilio Mutuo in San
Juan. It is Puerto Rico's only FACT-accredited cellular therapy program and performed the island's first CAR-T
infusion in August 2024. The brand speaks to three audiences at once: patients and families facing a blood
cancer diagnosis, the physicians who refer them, and the hospitals, manufacturers and partners who need an
accredited program on the island.

The voice is **the calm specialist**: expert, precise, serious and warm without being soft. It earns trust by
being specific where competitors are vague, by explaining rather than reassuring, and by refusing superlatives
it cannot prove. Its signature promise is geographic and emotional in one line: *"Bone marrow transplant and
cellular therapy — without leaving Puerto Rico."* Content should feel like a transplant physician sitting down
with you and telling you exactly what happens next, in your language.

Voice is constant; register is not. Spanish patient copy uses informal *tú*. Spanish copy to referring physicians
and institutions uses formal *usted*. This is a documented, intentional rule. The one exception is internal
documents addressed to TCT's own physicians, which the source material writes in *tú* as peer to peer.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **Expert and precise** — we name the modality, the accrediting body, the disease, the step | **Jargon-heavy or vague** — we never hide behind "advanced care" or unexplained acronyms |
| **Calm and reassuring** — we lower anxiety by explaining what happens next | **Alarmist, childish or saccharine** — no fear, no baby talk, no "journey" clichés |
| **Honest about claims** — every claim is verifiable or framed as process | **Hype or salesy** — no "world-class", "leading", "best", no outcome promises without data |
| **Rooted in Puerto Rico** — family, language and the Island are part of the value | **Generic or mainland-sounding** — we are not a franchise of a stateside center |
| **Collegial with physicians** — fast answers, their patient stays theirs | **Competitive or possessive** — we are a specialty program, not a replacement for the oncologist |
| **Plain and direct** — short sentences, one idea each, strong verbs | **Corporate** — no mission-statement prose, no committee language |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### 1. Expert and precise
- **What it means**: We demonstrate expertise through specificity, not adjectives. We say "autologous, allogeneic and haploidentical", we expand FACT on first use, we list what to send with a referral.
- **How it shows up**: Named modalities and diseases; accreditation explained as a process ("earned through on-site inspection... re-earned, not granted once"); concrete steps numbered 1 to 7.
- **What to avoid**: Unexplained acronyms in patient copy (HSCT, BMTCI, CIBMTR belong in institutional documents only). Adjective stacks ("advanced, innovative, cutting-edge").
- **Evidence**: Homepage copy deck sections 5, 6, 8, 9; Overview "Full-spectrum care" paragraph; AI manual principle "Clinical credibility: use FACT accreditation, specialist physician expertise, advanced services, and referral utility as real proof points."
- **Confidence**: High

#### 2. Calm and reassuring
- **What it means**: Patients arrive anxious. We reduce anxiety by making the path legible, not by telling them not to worry.
- **How it shows up**: "You will not be asked to decide before you understand." "If a document is missing, send the referral anyway — we will request what we need." "Every case is different, but the path is the same."
- **What to avoid**: Alarmist urgency aimed at patients; cutesy or childish tone; empty comfort ("you're in good hands").
- **Evidence**: Copy deck sections 9 and 10; AI manual Agent 6 tone: "Clear, Compassionate, Calm, Respectful, Not childish, Not alarmist, Not vague"; Agent 2 tone: "Expert, Reassuring, Clear, Compassionate, Serious, Modern, Not overly corporate, Not salesy."
- **Confidence**: High

#### 3. Honest about claims
- **What it means**: A claim is published only when it is verifiable (accreditation, location, services) or clearly framed as a description of process. Numbers are sourced or omitted.
- **How it shows up**: Copy deck marks every unverified figure `[VERIFY]`. Insurance copy says "covered benefits under most plans, subject to authorization", never "we guarantee coverage". Clinical outcome claims are absent by design.
- **What to avoid**: Superlatives without proof, placeholder statistics, service-level promises the team cannot meet, outcome percentages without cited program data.
- **Evidence**: Copy deck "Copy principles applied" and note: "A wrong number is worse than no number." AI manual: "Do not use weak filler language, generic marketing clichés, or unsupported superlatives."
- **Confidence**: High

#### 4. Rooted in Puerto Rico
- **What it means**: Staying home for treatment is a clinical and human advantage, and we say so plainly. Bilingual care is a feature, not a footnote.
- **How it shows up**: "without leaving Puerto Rico"; "close to your family, in your language"; "Patients here have historically traveled to the mainland for transplant — leaving family, work, and support behind at the hardest possible moment." In institutional copy: on-island capture rates, mainland repatriation, access-gap recovery.
- **What to avoid**: Treating Puerto Rico as a limitation to apologize for; English-first content with a thin Spanish translation; stateside idioms.
- **Evidence**: Copy deck hero and section 6; ARC analysis sections 3 and 7; AI manual principle "Puerto Rico context".
- **Confidence**: High

#### 5. Collegial with physicians
- **What it means**: Referring physicians are peers and partners. We promise speed, clarity and return of the patient with a documented plan.
- **How it shows up**: "You keep your patient." / "Su paciente sigue siendo suyo." "We evaluate, treat, and return patients to their referring physician with a clear plan." "Speak with a transplant physician" as a peer-to-peer CTA.
- **What to avoid**: Any implication that we take over care; marketing tone aimed at physicians; making them scroll past patient content to find the referral path.
- **Evidence**: Copy deck sections 6 and 8 and structural recommendation; AI manual Agent 3 tone: "Professional, Efficient, High-trust, Collegial, Clinically sophisticated, No fluff."
- **Confidence**: High

#### 6. Plain and direct
- **What it means**: Short sentences. One idea each. Headings that state the content. Strong verbs.
- **How it shows up**: "Blood cancer treatment is complex. Knowing where to start shouldn't be." "Transplant. CAR-T. Cellular therapy. Here." "It costs you nothing and it moves things forward."
- **What to avoid**: Corporate prose, mission-statement cadence, long compound sentences, passive voice in patient copy.
- **Evidence**: Copy deck throughout; AI manual "Write in plain, strong language... Use short paragraphs and clear headings."
- **Confidence**: High

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
Full-spectrum, FACT-accredited bone marrow transplant and cellular therapy, delivered in Puerto Rico so patients do not have to leave family, work and language behind.

Variations observed:
- "Bone marrow transplant and cellular therapy — without leaving Puerto Rico." (Copy deck hero, EN/ES)
- "Blood cancer treatment that doesn't require a plane ticket." (Copy deck alternative 1, emotional)
- "Puerto Rico's FACT-accredited bone marrow transplant and cellular therapy program." (Copy deck alternative 2, institutional/SEO)
- "Cellular Therapy & Bone Marrow Transplant in Puerto Rico" (Overview subtitle)
- "El Futuro de la Terapia Celular. Aquí en Puerto Rico." (Research one-pager, internal)

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Full spectrum, here**
   - Core idea: The only program in Puerto Rico offering every major modality: autologous, allogeneic (related, unrelated, haploidentical), CAR-T and bispecific antibody therapy.
   - When to use: Hero, services, partner and manufacturer materials.
   - Example phrasing: "One program, the full pathway — from evaluation through long-term follow-up."
   - Confidence: High (Overview, copy deck, website metadata)

2. **Accreditation that means something**
   - Core idea: FACT accreditation is inspected and re-earned; Authorized Treatment Center designations from Kite/Gilead and Bristol Myers Squibb; many insurers require it.
   - When to use: Credibility strip, "Why TCT", FAQ, every institutional document.
   - Example phrasing: "Accreditation is earned through on-site inspection of our clinical, laboratory, and collection practices — and it is re-earned, not granted once."
   - Confidence: High

3. **A team that does this every week**
   - Core idea: Six physicians formally trained in cellular therapy, supported by transplant-trained nursing, pharmacy and coordination. Transplant is what we do, not something we occasionally do.
   - When to use: "Why TCT", physician bios, referral page.
   - Example phrasing: "Transplant and cellular therapy are not occasional procedures for us."
   - Confidence: High

4. **Care that stays in Puerto Rico, in your language**
   - Core idea: Treatment close to family; consultations, consent, education and follow-up in Spanish and English "with the same level of detail in both."
   - When to use: Patient-facing copy, community and media.
   - Example phrasing: "Our program means most patients don't have to [travel]."
   - Confidence: High

5. **Connected back to your oncologist**
   - Core idea: We evaluate, treat and return the patient with a documented plan. A transplant physician reviews every referral; the published turnaround commitment is pending (see Open Question 2).
   - When to use: Referring-physician page, outreach to oncology practices.
   - Example phrasing: "You keep your patient."
   - Confidence: High

6. **Growth that does not cannibalize**
   - Core idea: For hospitals and partners, new TCT sites create net-new volume: on-island capture, mainland repatriation, access-gap recovery. Data-led, phased, confidential.
   - When to use: Hospital, blood-bank, manufacturer and investor documents only.
   - Example phrasing: "The BMTCI Program at CCCUPR does not cannibalize existing Puerto Rico hospital programs."
   - Confidence: Medium (single institutional document)

### Competitive Positioning
- **vs. Mainland US centers**: Equivalent accreditation and modalities, without the travel, the separation from caregivers, or the language barrier. Never disparage mainland centers; many physicians trained there.
- **vs. Other Puerto Rico hospitals**: The only FACT-accredited program and the only team performing allogeneic transplant and CAR-T on the island. State as verifiable fact, not as a boast.
- **vs. Status quo (no transplant / untreated)**: The "access gap" of patients who are eligible but never treated. Frame as the problem we exist to close.

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

Voice is constant. Tone flexes on formality, energy and technical depth. Spanish register is a fourth dimension unique to this brand.

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical Depth | Spanish register | Key Principle |
|---------|-----------|--------|-----------------|------------------|---------------|
| Patient & family web copy | Medium | Calm | Low-Medium, every term explained | *tú* | Legible path, no decision before understanding |
| Patient FAQ / microcopy | Medium-Low | Calm | Low | *tú* | Answer the real question in the first sentence |
| Referring-physician copy | Medium-High | Efficient | High | *usted* | Speed, clarity, "you keep your patient" |
| Hospital / partner / manufacturer documents | High | Measured | Very high, tables and phased plans | *usted* | Data over adjectives, "Confidential" footer |
| Internal physician-faculty strategy | Medium | Direct, blunt | High | *tú* (peer to peer; the documented exception) | Decision on one page, rules that protect us |
| Media / community | Medium | Warm | Low | *tú* or neutral | The Island, the family, the first CAR-T here |
| Social / education (Célula a Célula) | Low-Medium | Warm | Low-Medium | *tú* | Teach one thing clearly; never clickbait |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Patient and family copy
- **Overall tone**: Calm specialist. Serious, warm, never soft.
- **Opening approach**: State what we are and where, then the human benefit. "FACT-accredited... based at Hospital Auxilio Mutuo... close to your family, in your language."
- **Do's**: Number the steps. Expand every acronym. Plan for the caregiver. Offer the phone number as an always-visible utility CTA. Welcome second opinions.
- **Don'ts**: Outcome statistics without cited data. Guarantee insurance coverage. Use "journey", "battle", "warrior". Use *usted* with patients in Spanish.
- **Example**: "Te decimos qué tipo de trasplante o terapia celular corresponde a tu enfermedad, en qué consiste, cuáles son los riesgos y cuánto dura la recuperación. No se te va a pedir que decidas antes de entender."

#### Referring-physician copy
- **Overall tone**: Collegial, efficient, clinically sophisticated.
- **Opening approach**: Acknowledge that timing changes outcomes, then show the fastest path to an answer.
- **Do's**: List exactly what to send. State who to refer and when ("at diagnosis — not at relapse"). Promise a documented plan back. Use *usted*.
- **Don'ts**: Publish a response-time commitment that is not confirmed. Imply we take over the patient. Market.
- **Example**: "Sabemos que en trasplante y terapia celular el tiempo cambia los resultados. Nuestro proceso de referido está diseñado para darle una respuesta rápida."

#### Hospital, partner and manufacturer documents
- **Overall tone**: Institutional, data-first, confidential.
- **Opening approach**: Numbered sections, a "Purpose" block that says what the document demonstrates and for whom.
- **Do's**: Tables with ranges and sources. Phased timelines. Name the net effect on each stakeholder. Sign with name, title and email. Footer "Confidential".
- **Don'ts**: Patient-facing warmth. Unsourced market figures. Hedging adjectives.
- **Example**: "These volumes represent new activity generated by the program — not redistribution of existing ARC workload."

#### Internal physician-faculty strategy (Spanish)
- **Overall tone**: Direct, candid, decision-oriented. The one place the brand is allowed to be blunt.
- **Do's**: One-page decision format. "Por qué / Quién hace qué / Qué significa para cada médico / Las reglas que nos protegen." Name risks and precedents plainly.
- **Don'ts**: Circulate externally. Soften a risk to spare feelings.
- **Example**: "Todo entre entidades es por escrito y a valor de mercado. Nada se calcula por volumen de pacientes ni de referidos."

#### Social media and education
- **Overall tone**: Warm teacher. Confidence: Low — no published examples exist; derived from the AI manual's content-strategist brief.
- **Do's**: One clinical concept per post, explained for a family member. Bilingual by default. Physician thought leadership.
- **Don'ts**: Sensational or clickbait framing. Patient stories without consent process. Superlatives.

## Terminology Guide

### Must-Use Terms
| Term | Usage | Instead Of | Example |
|------|-------|------------|---------|
| FACT-accredited / acreditado por FACT | Always expand on first use: Foundation for the Accreditation of Cellular Therapy | "certified", "approved" | "We are accredited by FACT (Foundation for the Accreditation of Cellular Therapy)" |
| transplant and cellular therapy / trasplante y terapia celular | The paired phrase is the program's identity | "BMT program", "oncology clinic" | "a FACT-accredited transplant and cellular therapy program" |
| bone marrow transplant / trasplante de médula ósea | Patient-facing name for the procedure | "HSCT", "HCT" in patient copy | "Bone marrow transplant and cellular therapy — without leaving Puerto Rico." |
| autologous / allogeneic (autólogo / alogénico) | Always both, always explained once | "auto / allo" in patient copy | "Autologous transplant: your own stem cells are collected, stored, and returned to you" |
| CAR-T | Hyphenated in headlines, CTAs and patient copy | "CAR T", "CART" | "Transplant. CAR-T. Cellular therapy. Here." |
| referring physician / médico referidor | The physician audience | "provider", "doctor" when addressing physicians | "For referring physicians" |
| Hospital Auxilio Mutuo | Full name on first use, "Auxilio Mutuo" after | "HAM" in patient copy | "based at Hospital Auxilio Mutuo in San Juan" |

### Preferred Terms
| Term | Usage | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| the Island / la Isla (capitalized) | Warm reference to Puerto Rico in patient and community copy | "an accredited transplant program on the Island" |
| caregiver / cuidador | The family member who supports recovery | "We plan for a caregiver from the beginning, and we train that person" |
| second opinion / segunda opinión | Named service, always framed as welcome | "Second opinions are common and welcome" |
| blood cancers and blood disorders / cáncer y trastornos de la sangre | Umbrella for leukemia, lymphoma, myeloma, MDS, aplastic anemia | "leukemia, lymphoma, myeloma, and other blood disorders" |
| HSCT, HCT, auto-HCT, allo-HSCT, BMTCI, CIBMTR, HLA | Institutional and clinical documents only | "TCT has an established allogeneic HSCT program and CIBMTR reporting infrastructure" |
| CAR T-cell therapy | Acceptable long form on first mention in formal documents | "the first CAR T-cell therapy performed in Puerto Rico, in August 2024" |

### Avoid These Terms
| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|------|--------|-------------|
| world-class, leading, best, premier, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art | Unsupported superlatives; explicitly prohibited in copy principles | Name the specific proof: FACT, modalities, team |
| journey, battle, warrior, fight | Healthcare clichés; patronizing to patients | "process", "treatment", "recovery", "path" |
| guarantee (coverage, outcome) | Legal and ethical exposure | "covered benefits under most plans, subject to authorization" |
| provider (addressing physicians) | Impersonal; physicians are peers | "physician", "referring physician", "your oncologist" |
| compassionate care (as a slogan) | Generic; compassion must be shown, not claimed | Show it: "You will not be asked to decide before you understand." |
| clinic (for the program) | Undersells scope | "program" |

### Never-Use Terms
| Term | Reason |
|------|--------|
| Any unverified statistic or placeholder number | "A wrong number is worse than no number." Numbers ship only with program-record sign-off. |
| Outcome or survival percentages without cited program data | Prohibited by copy principles; regulatory and ethical risk |
| Response-time SLA that has not been confirmed by the clinical team | "Do not publish a service-level promise the team cannot meet" |
| Patient names or identifiable cases | Privacy; HIPAA |

## Language That Works

Ranked by how consistently the pattern recurs across sources. No conversational data was available, so effectiveness is inferred.

### Top Phrases
1. **"Bone marrow transplant and cellular therapy — without leaving Puerto Rico."** — Context: hero, media, anything first-touch. Why it works: names the service and the differentiator in one sentence, no adjectives.
2. **"You keep your patient." / "Su paciente sigue siendo suyo."** — Context: every physician touchpoint. Why it works: kills the main referral objection in four words.
3. **"Re-earned, not granted once."** — Context: explaining FACT. Why it works: turns a credential into a process a layperson can respect.
4. **"You will not be asked to decide before you understand."** — Context: process and consent copy. Why it works: reassurance delivered as a commitment, not a feeling.
5. **"A team that does this every week."** — Context: "Why TCT", bios. Why it works: expertise as frequency, not as adjective.
6. **"Close to your family, in your language." / "Cerca de tu familia y en tu idioma."** — Context: patient copy. Why it works: the two things the mainland cannot offer.
7. **"It costs you nothing and it moves things forward."** — Context: final CTA. Why it works: removes the two barriers to a first call.

### Questions That Engage
1. **"Do I have to leave Puerto Rico for a transplant?"** — Leads to: the core value proposition, answered "For most patients, no."
2. **"What is FACT accreditation and why does it matter?"** — Leads to: credibility explained plus the insurance angle.
3. **"Can my family be with me?"** — Leads to: caregiver planning, the brand's warmth shown through process.
4. **"When should I refer?"** (physician) — Leads to: "at diagnosis — not at relapse", positioning timing as the shared goal.

### Objection Handling Patterns
1. **Objection**: "I'll lose my patient if I refer." **Response pattern**: "We evaluate, treat, and return the patient to your care with a documented plan and direct access to our team." **Why it works**: concrete return path plus continued access.
2. **Objection**: "Isn't the mainland better?" **Response pattern**: Same accreditation standard, every modality, inside a full-service hospital, and the caregiver stays. **Why it works**: parity on quality, superiority on access. Never disparages.
3. **Objection**: "I don't have all the records." **Response pattern**: "Send the referral anyway — we will request what we need." **Why it works**: removes friction and signals ownership.
4. **Objection** (partner): "A new site will cannibalize our volume." **Response pattern**: Show the three real sources of volume and the net effect per hospital in a table. **Why it works**: data, not persuasion.

## Language to Avoid

### Anti-Patterns
1. **Superlative stacking** ("a world-class, leading-edge center of excellence") — Problem: unverifiable, generic, prohibited. Better: "Puerto Rico's FACT-accredited bone marrow transplant and cellular therapy program."
2. **Placeholder statistics** ("hundreds of successful transplants") — Problem: vague or wrong numbers destroy trust in a high-acuity brand. Better: a sourced figure with sign-off, or a non-numeric claim.
3. **Unexplained clinical shorthand in patient copy** ("allo-HSCT with RIC conditioning") — Problem: alienates the anxious reader. Better: "Stem cells from a matched donor — related, unrelated, or haploidentical."
4. **Formal *usted* with patients, informal *tú* with physicians** — Problem: breaks the documented register rule and reads as either cold or disrespectful. Better: *tú* for patients and families, *usted* for referring physicians and institutions. The one exception is internal documents to TCT's own physicians, which use *tú*.
5. **Coverage promises** ("your insurance will cover this") — Problem: legal exposure, broken trust at authorization. Better: "covered benefits under most plans, subject to authorization. Our team works through authorization with your plan."
6. **Marketing tone aimed at physicians** ("Discover the TCT difference!") — Problem: physicians evaluate expertise, not enthusiasm. Better: "A transplant physician reviews the case and responds with an eligibility assessment and proposed timeline."
7. **Translation-grade Spanish** (literal, English syntax) — Problem: bilingual care is a core promise; thin Spanish contradicts it. Better: write Spanish natively; the copy deck's Spanish is the standard.

## Content Examples

### Excellent Example — "Why TCT Oncology" block (copy deck, section 6)
> **Accreditation that means something.** We are accredited by FACT (Foundation for the Accreditation of Cellular Therapy), the international standard for transplant and cellular therapy programs. Accreditation is earned through on-site inspection of our clinical, laboratory, and collection practices — and it is re-earned, not granted once.
>
> **Care that stays in Puerto Rico.** Patients here have historically traveled to the mainland for transplant — leaving family, work, and support behind at the hardest possible moment. Our program means most patients don't have to.

**Why it is on-brand**: Each heading is a plain claim. Each body explains the mechanism (inspection, re-earning) or the human cost (family, work, support) rather than asserting quality. No superlatives. "Most patients" is honest scope, not "every patient".

### Excellent Example — Referring-physician close (copy deck, section 8, Spanish)
> **Su paciente sigue siendo suyo.** Evaluamos, tratamos y devolvemos al paciente a su cuidado con un plan documentado y acceso directo a nuestro equipo.

**Why it is on-brand**: Formal *usted* register, collegial, answers the objection before it is raised, promises a document and access rather than a feeling.

### Example to Avoid (constructed)
> Welcome to TCT Oncology, Puerto Rico's premier world-class destination for cutting-edge cancer care! Our compassionate team of leading experts has helped hundreds of warriors win their battle. Don't worry — your insurance has you covered. Start your journey today!

**What is wrong and how to fix it**: Five prohibited superlatives; an unsourced statistic; "warriors", "battle" and "journey" clichés; a coverage guarantee; exclamation-point energy that an anxious patient reads as sales. Rewrite: "TCT Oncology is a FACT-accredited transplant and cellular therapy program at Hospital Auxilio Mutuo in San Juan. We treat leukemia, lymphoma, myeloma, and other blood disorders with autologous transplant, allogeneic transplant, and CAR-T therapy — close to your family, in your language. Call [phone] or request an appointment."
