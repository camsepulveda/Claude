## Executive Summary

Health Front is a Puerto Rico health services administration firm, founded in 2019, that positions itself as
"Healthcare Strategy · Regulatory Advisory · Operations." Its clients are physician groups, primary care
centers and hospitals. Its best-known service line is a concierge credentialing and licensing program that gets
specialist physicians licensed, privileged and contracted with Medicare, Medicaid and insurers faster than they
could manage alone. Its stated mission is to close gaps in Puerto Rico's health system, and its recurring
purpose statement is to keep specialist physicians on the island.

The voice has two eras in the record. The 2023 to 2024 documents are earnest, warm and mission-forward, but
template-like and occasionally loose ("we will do this efficiently and quickly as possible"). The 2026 proposal
is a different animal: tightly structured, fixed-fee, single point of accountability, with explicit
assumptions, exclusions and a plain statement of what Health Front is not. These guidelines codify the 2026
voice as the standard because it is the most recent and the only CEO-signed document. The 2026 proposal drops
the mission language entirely; these guidelines carry it forward from 2023 anyway, as the "why," because it is the
only stated purpose in the record and it recurs verbatim across two years. Whether to keep it is Open Question 3.

The voice to aim for is **the accountable operator**: precise about scope, candid about limits, procedural
without being cold, and visibly motivated by Puerto Rico's physician shortage.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **Accountable** — one point of contact, a fixed fee, a tracker, promises we keep | **Vague about ownership** — no "we will assist" without saying who does what |
| **Procedurally precise** — numbered workstreams, named agencies and forms, weekly follow-up | **Loose** — no "as quickly as possible", no unnumbered task lists |
| **Candid about scope and limits** — assumptions, exclusions, "not legal counsel" | **Over-promising** — we never imply we can shorten an agency's clock |
| **Burden-removing (concierge)** — we carry the paperwork so the physician can practice | **Transactional** — not a form-filling service; we represent the physician |
| **Mission-rooted in Puerto Rico** — closing gaps, keeping specialists on the island | **Generic consultancy** — no "foster high-performing physician enterprises" boilerplate |
| **Data-driven** — trackers, timelines, phase status | **Anecdotal** — no "in our experience it usually works out" |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### 1. Accountable
- **What it means**: Health Front takes the file and owns the outcome. The client knows who is responsible and can see status at any time.
- **How it shows up**: "fixed-fee, single-point-of-accountability engagement"; "receive all IDFPR correspondence on behalf of the physician, and respond to deficiency notices within 72 hours"; a web-based tracker and biweekly status emails; "We keep our promises and commitments" (2023 values).
- **What to avoid**: Passive constructions that hide the actor; "support" or "assist" without a named deliverable.
- **Evidence**: IL proposal sections 2, 3, 7; CC Program deck "Personal Compliance Agent... acts as a mediator and physician representative... for the year"; 2023 values "Accountability".
- **Confidence**: Medium (consistent across 4 documents, one explicit)

#### 2. Procedurally precise
- **What it means**: Expertise is demonstrated by naming the agency, the form, the sequence and the dependency, not by claiming experience.
- **How it shows up**: "IDFPR Form 036 (Licensure by Endorsement)"; "Junta de Licenciamiento y Disciplina Médica"; "Medicare PTAN, Medicaid PEP"; workstream tables; week-by-week timelines with status tags.
- **What to avoid**: Unnumbered bullet soup; agencies referred to generically ("the board"); skipping the client's own responsibilities.
- **Evidence**: IL proposal throughout; licensing and telemedicine proposals list every document by its official name; pre-assessment form's credential matrix.
- **Confidence**: Medium

#### 3. Candid about scope and limits
- **What it means**: We say what is included, what is excluded, what we assume, and what we are not.
- **How it shows up**: Dedicated "Assumptions" and "Exclusions" sections; "Typical end-to-end timeline is 8 to 12 weeks from filing, and the dominant driver of delay is incomplete or out-of-sequence submissions"; "This engagement does not constitute the provision of legal representation."
- **What to avoid**: Letting a client infer we control an agency's processing time; burying exclusions.
- **Evidence**: IL proposal sections 1, 6, 7, 8. Partially present in 2023-2024 proposals as "Client responsibility" sections.
- **Confidence**: Medium (strong in 2026 document, weaker earlier)

#### 4. Burden-removing (concierge)
- **What it means**: The physician's job is to practice medicine. Ours is everything administrative between them and a paying, privileged, licensed practice.
- **How it shows up**: "We function as a concierge company"; "eliminate the administrative burden on the physician and the recruiting practice"; "We serve as a mediator between the accountant, biller, hospital, medical group, and insurance companies."
- **What to avoid**: Making the physician do our sequencing; jargon that forces them to look things up.
- **Evidence**: CC Program deck "Solution" and "How it works"; IL proposal section 1.
- **Confidence**: Medium

#### 5. Mission-rooted in Puerto Rico
- **What it means**: The company exists because specialists leave the island over administrative friction. Every engagement is framed as part of fixing that.
- **How it shows up**: "We are committed to serving the communities of Puerto Rico with growth, resources, and advancement in healthcare" (appears verbatim in 2023 and 2024); "closing the gaps in health care services" (vision); "solve much of the reasons why medical specialists unfortunately decide to leave the island."
- **What to avoid**: Treating the mission as a closing paragraph only; mission language in fee tables.
- **Evidence**: Corp presentation mission and vision; CC Program deck introduction and primary goals; licensing proposal cover letter.
- **Confidence**: Medium (explicit, but the 2026 proposal drops it entirely — see open question 3)

#### 6. Data-driven
- **What it means**: Status, cost and progress are visible and measured.
- **How it shows up**: "real-time HTML project tracker shared with client"; "Automated Dash-Board Reporting"; 2023 value "Data Driven Culture"; diagnosis of Puerto Rico's "lack of data driven culture" as a system gap.
- **What to avoid**: Status updates by phone only; promises without a milestone.
- **Evidence**: IL proposal section 2 workstream 7; corp presentation values and services.
- **Confidence**: Medium

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
Health Front removes the administrative, regulatory and credentialing burden that stands between a physician or practice and a licensed, privileged, reimbursed operation in Puerto Rico, on a fixed fee with a single point of accountability.

Variations observed:
- "Healthcare Strategy · Regulatory Advisory · Operations" (2026 letterhead tagline)
- "a fixed-fee, single-point-of-accountability engagement designed to compress the licensing timeline and eliminate the administrative burden" (2026 IL proposal)
- "Health Front provides solutions for many critical aspects in moving your entire practice to Puerto Rico" (2023, 2024 proposals, verbatim)
- "a health services administration firm with the mission to exceed expectations in providing top of the line service, outstanding quality and true value to our client partners and the patients they serve" (2023 mission)

### Key Message Pillars

1. **One point of accountability**
   - Core idea: Health Front is the physician's authorized representative; agencies talk to us, the client gets a tracker.
   - When to use: Every proposal, the website services page.
   - Example phrasing: "single point of accountability"; "Health Front will act as the physician's authorized licensing representative."
   - Confidence: Medium

2. **We know the sequence**
   - Core idea: Delay comes from out-of-order or incomplete filings; we sequence correctly the first time.
   - When to use: Engagement context sections, objection handling on "why not do it ourselves."
   - Example phrasing: "the dominant driver of delay is incomplete or out-of-sequence submissions."
   - Confidence: Medium (explicit in one document, implicit in the rest)

3. **Fixed fee, clear terms**
   - Core idea: Professional fee stated once; pass-through costs itemized; 50/50 payment terms; exclusions listed.
   - When to use: Every proposal.
   - Example phrasing: "Total estimated client outlay (professional fee + pass-through)."
   - Confidence: Medium (pattern across all four proposals)

4. **Keeping specialists in Puerto Rico**
   - Core idea: Administrative friction drives physician flight; solving it is economic development and patient access.
   - When to use: Cover letters, about page, pitch decks, public statements. Not fee tables.
   - Example phrasing: "committed to serving the communities of Puerto Rico with growth, resources, and advancement in healthcare."
   - Confidence: Medium (explicit, dated 2023 to 2024)

5. **Beyond credentialing: strategy and operations**
   - Core idea: Contracting, revenue cycle, managed-care consulting, M&A, strategic planning, new practice setup.
   - When to use: Institutional pitches, website services.
   - Example phrasing: 2023 service list.
   - Confidence: Low (listed once in 2023; no later evidence these lines are actively sold)

### Competitive Positioning
- **vs. Doing it in-house**: The physician or office manager does not know the sequence, cannot chase five agencies weekly, and loses 6 to 9 months of billing (figure from the 2024 CC Program deck; re-verify before quoting). State the timeline cost, not our effort.
- **vs. Generic credentialing vendors**: Health Front is Puerto Rico-based, led by a licensed health administrator with legal training (never acting as counsel), and represents the physician before the agency rather than filling forms. Bilingual delivery is a plausible differentiator but is unverified in the sources (Open Question 5). Evidence is thin; use carefully.
- **vs. Status quo (leaving the island)**: The mission frame. Use in public and recruiting content.

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Client proposals
- **Overall tone**: Professional services. Formal, exact, complete.
- **Opening approach**: Letterhead tagline, proposal number, "Engagement Summary" box in two or three sentences stating what we will do end to end.
- **Do's**: Number the sections. Table the workstreams. Separate professional fee from pass-through costs. State assumptions and exclusions. State what we are not (legal counsel). Thirty-day acceptance window. Sign with full name, credentials, title.
- **Don'ts**: "as quickly as possible"; blanks left for the client name in a final PDF; promising agency timelines as if ours.
- **Example**: "Health Front offers a fixed-fee, single-point-of-accountability engagement designed to compress the licensing timeline and eliminate the administrative burden on the physician and the recruiting practice."

#### Cover letters and first contact
- **Overall tone**: Warm, direct, brief.
- **Do's**: Thank for the opportunity in one sentence. State the mission in one sentence. Offer a direct phone number.
- **Don'ts**: Generic enthusiasm ("we are excited to partner"); more than three short paragraphs.
- **Example**: "We are committed to serving the communities of Puerto Rico with growth, resources, and advancement in healthcare. This is one of the important reasons we want to help bring your expertise and practice to the island."

#### Pitch decks
- **Overall tone**: Confident, problem-led.
- **Do's**: Problem slide with the real cost (6 to 9 months unpaid while contracting, per the 2024 deck). Solution as a named program. "How it works" in four numbered steps. Team with credentials.
- **Don'ts**: Values slides lifted from boilerplate; service lists without proof.

#### Status updates
- **Overall tone**: Efficient. Confidence: Low — no examples in sources; derived from the tracker commitment.
- **Do's**: Phase, what moved, what is blocked, what we need from the client by when.

## Language That Works

Inferred from written documents; no conversational data.

### Top Phrases
1. **"Fixed-fee, single-point-of-accountability engagement."** — Context: engagement summaries, website. Why it works: three buyer anxieties (cost, ownership, chasing) answered in one phrase.
2. **"The dominant driver of delay is incomplete or out-of-sequence submissions."** — Context: engagement context, objection handling. Why it works: diagnoses the problem as process, which is what we sell.
3. **"Committed to serving the communities of Puerto Rico with growth, resources, and advancement in healthcare."** — Context: cover letters, about page. Why it works: the one mission line that survived from 2023 into 2024 unchanged.
4. **"We keep our promises and commitments."** — Context: values, team page. Why it works: plain, falsifiable, memorable.
5. **"Eliminate the administrative burden on the physician and the recruiting practice."** — Context: proposals to groups hiring a physician. Why it works: names both buyers.

### Questions That Engage
1. **"How long until the physician can bill?"** — Leads to: the 6 to 9 month contracting gap (2024 deck figure) and the CC Program.
2. **"Who is responsible if the agency sends a deficiency notice?"** — Leads to: authorized representative, 72-hour response.
3. **"What is not included?"** — Leads to: exclusions section; builds trust by answering before asked.

### Objection Handling Patterns
1. **Objection**: "Our office manager can do this." **Response pattern**: Name the agencies and sequence involved, state the typical timeline and what drives delay, quantify the months of lost billing. **Why it works**: converts effort into cost.
2. **Objection**: "What if it is denied?" **Response pattern**: "Engagement does not include legal representation in the event of a denial or formal disciplinary inquiry, which would be quoted separately." **Why it works**: candor about limits is itself the trust signal.

## Language to Avoid

### Anti-Patterns
1. **Template residue** (blank underscores for the client name, "Dear Dr. ____" in a final PDF) — Problem: signals mass production; found in two 2023-2024 files. Better: every proposal personalized and proposal-numbered as in 2026.
2. **Speed without a number** ("efficiently and quickly as possible") — Problem: vague and ungrammatical. Better: "Typical end-to-end timeline is 8 to 12 weeks from filing."
3. **Boilerplate consultancy prose** ("design, develop, and implement solutions that foster high-performing physician organizations") — Problem: could be any firm. Better: list the actual service and the actual agency.
4. **Mission as filler** (mission paragraph only in the conclusion) — Problem: reads as decoration. Better: one mission sentence up front in cover letters; none in fee sections.
5. **Inconsistent identity** (Health Front LLC, HealthFront, HF Group, Health Front Group; healthfrontpr.com and healthfrontgroup.com; "Consultant - HF Group", "Executive Director", "Managing Member & CEO") — Problem: undermines the accountability attribute. Better: one name, one domain, one title per person. See open questions 1, 2, 7.

## Content Examples

### Excellent Example — 2026 proposal engagement summary
> Health Front LLC will manage end-to-end the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR) licensure-by-endorsement application for one (1) Puerto Rico–licensed cardiologist. The engagement is delivered on a fixed-fee basis and covers credentials assembly, FCVS profile coordination, third-party verifications, fingerprint logistics, CORE portal filing, and deficiency cure through license issuance.

**Why it is on-brand**: One sentence of scope, one of commercial terms. Agency named and expanded. Every workstream listed by its real name. "Through license issuance" states the finish line.

### Excellent Example — Limits stated plainly
> This engagement does not constitute the provision of legal representation, and Health Front is not acting as legal counsel to the physician or the practice. Where formal legal counsel is required, Health Front will coordinate with the client's preferred attorney.

**Why it is on-brand**: Candor about limits plus a path forward. Protects the client and the firm.

### Example to Avoid (from 2023-2024 templates, lightly paraphrased)
> With our expertise in credentialing and compliance, we will do this efficiently and quickly as possible. Health Front provides solutions for many critical aspects... With our expertise, experience, relationships, and diligence, we will make this process efficient and expeditious. We are excited to work as a team with you.

**What is wrong and how to fix it**: "quickly as possible" is vague and ungrammatical; "expertise, experience, relationships, and diligence" is an adjective list with no proof; "excited" is filler; the same paragraph appears verbatim in two unrelated proposals. Rewrite: "Health Front will act as your authorized representative before the Junta de Licenciamiento y Disciplina Médica, file the complete application in the correct sequence, follow up weekly, and deliver the issued license. Typical processing is [X] weeks from filing; the fee is fixed at $[X] plus itemized agency costs."
